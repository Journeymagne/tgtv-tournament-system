## WTC · 3×3

1. Both captains roll D6, rerolling ties. The winner is Attacker; the loser is Defender.

2. The match has a shared pool of nine missions. Defender bans one, then Attacker bans another, leaving seven.

3. Both captains secretly select a Shield from their own team. Choices are revealed after both captains confirm.

4. Each captain secretly chooses an opposing Sword for their own Shield from the other team's two Swords. Both choices are revealed together. The remaining Swords form the third pairing.

5. Defender's Shield pairing: Attacker chooses a table; Defender chooses an available mission.

6. Attacker's Shield pairing: Defender chooses one of the two remaining tables; Attacker chooses a different available mission.

7. The remaining Swords automatically receive the last table. Defender chooses another available mission for them. All three tables and missions are distinct. Deployment belongs to the table.

8. Three individual games are created. TTS uses the round's three shared virtual tables; IRL uses the match's own table line.

## Sword Shield Classic · 3×3

1. Determine initiative by rolling dice or another agreed method and record the winning captain in the app. There are no mission bans; use the round's mission pool.

2. Both captains secretly select a Shield from their own team. Choices are revealed after both captains confirm.

3. Each captain secretly chooses an opposing Sword for their own Shield from the other team's two Swords. Both choices are revealed together. The remaining Swords form the third pairing.

4. The captain with initiative chooses EITHER a table OR a mission for their own Shield. The other captain assigns the other parameter to that pairing.

5. The captain without initiative chooses EITHER a table OR a mission for their own Shield. The captain with initiative assigns the other parameter.

6. The remaining Swords receive the last table. With exactly three missions, the last mission is automatic; with a larger pool, the captain without initiative chooses it.

7. All pairings use distinct tables and missions. Deployment belongs to the table. Three individual games are created after all assignments.

## Doubles · 2×2

1. Roll D6 outside the app, rerolling ties. The winner chooses either pairing players or assigning tables. The loser receives the other right.

2. The captain entitled to pair players sets the two individual matchups. The other captain assigns the two tables from the match's line.

3. The roll-off loser chooses one of the round's four missions for either table. The winner chooses one of the remaining three for the second table.

4. Enter both pairings, distinct tables and distinct missions in the manual form. Each player participates once. Clear an occupied choice before swapping.

5. Either captain or the organizer saves both pairings, creating two individual games. This format has no Shield/Sword selections or bans.

## Manual assignments · 3×3

1. Step-by-step captain pairing is disabled. Captains agree on three pairings, tables and missions outside the app. There are no initiative, ban or hidden-selection stages in the interface.

2. A captain or organizer enters one player from each team for each of the three games. Every player participates exactly once.

3. Assign a distinct table and an available distinct mission to each pairing. Deployment belongs to the table.

4. Review and save all three pairings to create the individual games.

1. This match retains the previous rules version. Captains roll off: the winner is Attacker, the loser Defender. There is no ban stage.

2. Both captains secretly select a Shield from their own team. Choices are revealed after both captains confirm.
